Success is fragile in many ways; perfection is an illusion. We must therefore act ahead of certainty, remembering that *[faith without works is dead](https://www.esv.org/James+2:17/)* (James 2:17).

At inflection points, faith becomes vigilance made visible—relentless work carried out in strategic dissonance: *[“I believe; help my unbelief”](https://www.esv.org/Mark+9:24/)* (Mark 9:24).

Kierkegaard described this as the courage to act without immediate guarantees, drawing on *[Fear and Trembling](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/)*, while Nietzsche warned that survival belongs to those disciplined enough to endure tension, as discussed in *[Beyond Good and Evil](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nietzsche/)*.

That tension is not merely a catalyst for growth—it is the condition for survival.

Only the paranoid survive.

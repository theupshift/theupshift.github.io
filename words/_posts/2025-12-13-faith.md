Success is fragile in many ways; perfection is an illusion. We must therefore act ahead of certainty, remembering that *faith without works is dead* (James 2:17).

At inflection points, faith becomes vigilance made visible—relentless work carried out in strategic dissonance: *“I believe; help my unbelief”* (Mark 9:24).

Kierkegaard described this as the courage to act without immediate guarantees, while Nietzsche warned that survival belongs to those disciplined enough to endure tension.

That tension is not merely a catalyst for growth—it is the condition for survival.

Only the paranoid survive.
